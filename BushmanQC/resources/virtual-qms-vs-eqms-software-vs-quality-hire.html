Virtual QMS vs. eQMS Software vs. a Full-Time Quality Hire: Which Does a Startup Need?

An honest decision guide: what each option costs, how long it takes, who carries the Part 11 validation burden, and how the three combine as you grow.

By Nellie Bushman, Quality Expert & Founder Published October 8, 2026 Last reviewed October 8, 2026 ~10 min read

Short answer: Most pre-revenue device startups with a small team are best served by a consultant-built Virtual QMS on compliant cloud storage, run with fractional expert support. Move to eQMS software when users, record volume or partner expectations outgrow that setup, and hire a full-time quality manager when quality work fills a full week, usually around commercial manufacturing. The options aren't exclusive: Many growing companies end up with eQMS software, a quality hire, and outside expertise for audits and gaps.

Founders usually frame this as a purchasing decision: Which software do we buy, or which person do we hire? That framing skips a step. Under the QMSR, what FDA requires is a documented quality management system that complies with ISO 13485 and FDA's additional requirements, per 21 CFR 820.10(a)1. Software doesn't satisfy that on its own, and neither does a new employee. Each of the three options below solves a different part of the problem, which is why the honest answer is often "two of them, in sequence."

What are you actually choosing between?

Option A: A consultant-built Virtual QMS

An expert designs and documents your QMS (Quality Manual, procedures, forms, logs, training, CAPA, supplier and design controls) and builds it on general-purpose, compliant cloud storage with a Part 11 electronic signature workflow. You get the system and the tooling at once, sized for a small team, with no proprietary platform underneath. Ongoing upkeep is done by your team, with fractional support from the consultant if you want it.

Option B: eQMS software (SaaS)

A purpose-built quality platform such as Greenlight Guru, Qualio, SimplerQMS or MasterControl. You get pre-built workflows for document control, training, CAPA, complaints and audits, plus traceability features and vendor-supplied validation documentation. You don't get your procedures: Someone still has to decide how your company handles design changes, nonconformances and suppliers, and write that down.

Option C: A full-time quality manager

An employee who owns quality day to day. You get continuous attention, an internal management representative, and someone on site for suppliers, production and inspections. You don't automatically get a system: A new quality manager still has to build or buy one, which takes them away from operating it.

What does each option cost in year one?

Virtual QMS

The main cost is the implementation project, commonly quoted between $10,000 and $75,000+ depending on device class, team size and how much is customized. The tools themselves are inexpensive because they're general-purpose cloud services you may already pay for, plus a compliant e-signature layer. If you keep a consultant on a small fractional retainer afterward, that becomes a predictable monthly line.

eQMS software

Some vendors publish starting prices and some don't. As of October 8, 2026:

  • Greenlight Guru states that its eQMS packages start at $12,000 per year, with implementation quoted separately2.
  • SimplerQMS lists a Starter plan "starting from USD 17,500 annually" for up to 15 users; larger plans are quote-based3.
  • Qualio does not publish prices; its Foundation, Growth and Scale plans are quoted after a conversation, and it states that an implementation fee applies to all plans4.
  • MasterControl does not publish prices and asks buyers to contact sales for tailored pricing5.

Budget for user growth, add-on modules and implementation on top of the base subscription, and ask every vendor for a three-year total. Prices change often, so check each vendor's current page.

Full-time quality manager

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics groups quality control systems managers with industrial production managers, whose median annual wage was $126,060 in May 2025, with the lowest 10% earning under $78,000 and the highest 10% over $205,5206. Salary is only part of the cost. In June 2026, benefits made up 30.0% of employer compensation costs for private industry workers7. If benefits run at that share, a $126,060 salary implies roughly $180,000 a year in total compensation, before recruiting fees, equipment, and the software the hire will want.

Part 11 and validation: who carries the burden?

All three options land in the same place: If your quality records are electronic, 21 CFR Part 118 applies, and § 11.109 places its controls (validation, audit trails, access limits, trained users) on the persons who use the system. No vendor can make you compliant by itself, and no tool choice exempts you.

What differs is how much of the work you start with. FDA's final Computer Software Assurance (CSA) guidance10, issued February 3, 2026, lets manufacturers leverage validation activities performed by other entities, such as developers, suppliers and cloud service providers, and ties the rigor of validation to risk, as ISO 13485 subclauses 4.1.6, 7.5.6 and 7.6 require. For cloud storage that holds quality records, the guidance recommends focusing assurance on the features relevant to record integrity and the Part 11 requirements that apply to those records.

  • Virtual QMS: You (or your consultant) write a risk-based validation record for the configured storage and signature tools, leaning on the cloud provider's documentation where it exists. The scope is narrow because the tools do a narrow job.
  • eQMS software: The vendor typically ships validation documentation with each release. Greenlight Guru, SimplerQMS and MasterControl all describe validation documentation or tools as included235. That's a real head start. You still need to assess the vendor as a supplier and confirm the system works for your intended use, which the CSA guidance describes as a risk-based vendor analysis10.
  • Quality hire: The burden moves to whichever system they build or buy, plus their time.

When is eQMS software the right call?

eQMS software is the right tool for many device companies, and pretending otherwise doesn't help anyone. It earns its subscription when:

  • More people sign records than one person can track by hand. Automated routing, reminders and overdue-task reports start paying for themselves.
  • Record volume climbs. Complaints, nonconformances, CAPAs and supplier records from commercial production are where manual workflows strain first.
  • Design traceability gets complex. Many requirements, risks and tests across product variants are easier to manage with built-in trace matrices.
  • Partners, acquirers or a notified body expect it. Commercial expectations are a legitimate reason.
  • You have someone to run it. Software without an owner is an expensive filing cabinet.

The order matters. Design your processes first, then configure the software to match them. Teams that buy the platform before deciding how they work usually end up configuring it twice.

When should you hire a full-time quality manager?

Hire when quality work fills a real week, every week. That usually means commercial manufacturing with suppliers to monitor, a steady stream of complaints and CAPAs, routine production records, and the prospect of FDA inspections or registrar surveillance audits on a regular cycle. A full-time hire is also the natural internal management representative once the company is large enough to support one.

Two warnings. First, a first quality hire in a small company has to be both architect and operator, and those are different skills, so plan which one you're hiring for. Second, a new hire who inherits a well-built system is productive in weeks; one who starts from nothing spends months building before they can run anything.

The options combine, and usually should

Most companies use more than one of these over their life. A common progression looks like this:

StageCommon setup
Prototype / pre-design-freezeConsultant-built Virtual QMS, run by founders with fractional support
Pre-submission (510(k) / De Novo)Virtual QMS fully operating; fractional support; evaluate eQMS if the team is growing
Cleared / first commercial saleseQMS configured to your existing procedures, first quality hire, consultant for audits, gap assessments and surges

Consultant plus eQMS is a perfectly good combination at any stage, as long as the consultant designs the processes and the software is configured to them, not the other way around. Moving from a Virtual QMS to an eQMS later is normal: Quality records are portable when document control has been disciplined from the start.

A five-question decision test

  1. Who will operate the QMS next month, and for how many hours a week? If the answer is "a founder, a few hours," you need a system sized for that, not a platform sized for a quality department.
  2. How many people sign quality records? A handful can work well with a Virtual QMS; a growing headcount is the clearest sign software will pay off.
  3. Are you manufacturing finished devices for sale yet? Commercial production drives record volume and inspection exposure, which is when a quality hire becomes justifiable.
  4. Is your cash better spent on a system now or a salary now? A one-time build plus a small retainer is a very different burn rate from a six-figure salary.
  5. Has anyone designed your processes yet? If not, that's the first job, whichever tool or person you choose.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Virtual QMS on general-purpose cloud storage really FDA compliant?

It can be. FDA regulates the controls, not the brand of software: validated tools, access tied to named individuals, a protected audit trail, and compliant electronic signatures. FDA's CSA guidance explicitly addresses cloud storage used for quality records10. Implemented casually, the same tools are just shared folders. See our FAQ on Part 11.

Does buying eQMS software make us Part 11 compliant?

No. It gives you features that support compliance and, often, vendor validation documentation. Compliance still depends on how you configure, validate and use the system9.

Can a consultant replace a quality hire?

Early on, often yes, for the building and part-time operating. Not forever: As production and headcount grow, you need someone inside the company, and ISO 13485 clause 5.5.2 calls for a member of management to serve as management representative (the standard is free to read in the ANSI incorporation-by-reference reading room)11.

Can we start on a Virtual QMS and move to eQMS software later?

Yes, and many companies do. The procedures, forms and records you build don't belong to a tool. If document control has been disciplined (consistent numbering, clear revision history, complete approvals), migration is mostly a data-entry and configuration project, followed by validation of the new system for your intended use. Plan it for a quiet stretch, not the month before a submission or an audit.
